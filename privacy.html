Privacy Policy

Spendly for Android and iOS

Last updated: 26 August 2026

The short version

Spendly is a local-first expense tracker. Your financial records — transactions, budgets, accounts, goals, notes, tags and receipt photos — are stored on your device, and the app is fully usable with no account and no connection at all.

We cannot read your transactions. That holds whether or not you use sync, and it is a statement about mathematics rather than about our intentions: if you turn sync on, each record is encrypted on your phone before it is uploaded, using a key derived from a passphrase that is never sent to us. What our servers hold is unreadable to us.

There is no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting, and we never sell or share your information.

Two features use the internet, and both are off until you turn them on:

  • Sync — signs you in and stores an encrypted copy of your records so you can restore them or use a second device.
  • Money reading — fetches public finance headlines. It carries nothing about you and never involves your transactions.

Everything else — every calculation, every scan, every local backup — works with no connection.

If you forget your sync passphrase, the encrypted copy cannot be recovered. Not by you, and not by us: we do not have the key, which is the whole point. Keep using Back up (JSON) as well.

1. Who we are

Spendly is published by Appricity. For any privacy question or request, contact appricity.support@gmail.com.

2. What Spendly stores, and where

Everything you enter stays in a private database inside the app's own storage area on your device:

  • Transactions (expenses, income, transfers) including amounts, dates, notes, tags, merchant/payee names and split allocations
  • Categories, budgets, savings goals and accounts, including opening balances
  • Recurring rules
  • App preferences (theme, currency, haptics, App Lock, notification settings)
  • Receipt photos you choose to attach, stored as files in the app's own directory

With sync off, none of this leaves your device.

With sync on, a copy is stored on our servers (Google Cloud Firestore) — but encrypted before it leaves your phone, with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from your passphrase using Argon2id. We hold the ciphertext and nothing else. The passphrase is never transmitted, so we cannot decrypt any of it. Not the amount, the payee, the category, the transaction date, the note, or any tag.

What the server can see about a record, because these are needed to merge changes between devices without reading them, is:

  • its type — expense, goal, and so on — and therefore how many of each you have;
  • when it was last changed, when it was uploaded, and whether it is deleted;
  • its internal record id. These ids embed the moment the record was created, so for entries you add as you spend, the creation time is a rough proxy for when the transaction happened. The amount, payee and category remain encrypted;
  • for categories and accounts, the id is a stable name such as food — so the server can tell which built-in categories you kept, but not what you spent in any of them;
  • the approximate size of each record.

We would rather spell that out than claim the stored data is completely opaque. The contents are unreadable to us; the shape of the ledger is not entirely.

Receipt photos are not synced in this version. They stay on your device.

Spendly also opts out of Android's own backup system, so the operating system does not copy your ledger to Google Drive either. Moving your data to a new phone is something you choose to do — with a JSON backup, or with sync.

3. What we collect

With sync off: nothing. Spendly contains no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting, no telemetry and no tracking of any kind. We do not collect device identifiers, advertising IDs, usage statistics or diagnostics.

If you sign in, we necessarily receive:

  • the identifier you signed in with — your email address, or your phone number, or the Google account you chose;
  • a user id that Firebase assigns you;
  • if you enable sync, the encrypted copy of your records described above, plus the salt needed to re-derive your key on another device.

Our sign-in provider (Firebase Authentication) also keeps its own security logs, which include the IP address a sign-in came from. We do not build a profile, and there is no automated decision-making. We never sell or share personal information.

(If you turn on the optional Money reading section, your device contacts news publishers directly to download their public headlines. Those publishers see the request the way any website does. Nothing about you is added to it, and nothing comes back to us — see section 6.)

3a. Deleting your data

Turning sync off offers to delete the cloud copy, and choosing that removes every record and your key material from our servers. Your device keeps everything either way. To delete the account itself, email us at the address in section 1.

5. Things you share into Spendly

You can hand Spendly a single item to save you typing:

  • Share a message to Spendly — from your messaging app, share a bank or payment alert. Spendly reads the amount, merchant, date and reference out of it on your device and shows you a draft to confirm.
  • Scan a receipt or screenshot — photograph a receipt, or pick a payment screenshot. The text is recognised on your device by a model built into the app, not by a service.
  • Paste text into Quick Add.

Three things are true of all of these:

  1. Spendly only ever sees the single item you hand it. It has no ability to read your inbox, your notifications or your photo library, and it does no background scanning. Nothing reaches Spendly unless you share it.
  2. Recognition and parsing happen entirely on your phone. The message you share and the image you scan are never transmitted, and neither is the text read out of them. If the resulting transaction is later synced, it travels encrypted like any other record.
  3. Nothing is saved without you. Every capture is presented as a draft you confirm, edit or discard. Spendly never writes to your records on its own.

Images you scan are stored only if you keep them as a receipt attached to a transaction, in the app's own private folder, exactly like a receipt you attach by hand.

6. Money reading (optional, off by default)

Spendly can show a list of finance headlines from public news feeds. It is off until you turn it on in Settings → Money reading.

What is sent. A plain request for each publisher's public headline list — the same request your browser makes when it loads a page. It carries no account, no device identifier, no advertising ID, no cookies and none of your financial data. We do not add anything to it. As with any internet request, the publisher's server sees the IP address it came from; that is inherent to downloading anything and is not something Spendly collects, receives or stores.

What is stored. The headlines themselves are cached on your device so the section works offline. Turning the feature off deletes that cache.

Reading an article opens it in your own browser, not inside Spendly. From that point the publisher's own privacy policy applies — Spendly is not involved and cannot see what you read.

Current sources: Mint, Economic Times, Business Standard and MarketWatch.

We receive nothing from any of this. There is no server of ours in the path.

7. Files you create yourself

Spendly can export your data at your request: CSV export, JSON backup and PDF report are written to a temporary file and handed to your device's share sheet. CSV and backup import read from a file you pick.

Once you share a file, where it goes is determined by the app you send it to (email, cloud drive, messaging) and is governed by that app's privacy policy, not this one. We never see these files.

8. Data retention and deletion

Your data stays until you remove it. You are always in control:

  • Delete any individual record; it moves to Recently deleted and is purged automatically after 30 days, or immediately if you empty it
  • Settings → Erase all data permanently deletes everything
  • Uninstalling the app removes all of its local data

With sync off, we hold no copy, so there is nothing to request from us and nothing for us to delete on your behalf.

With sync on, there is a copy, and you can remove it:

  • Settings → Turn off sync → "Turn off and delete" erases the encrypted copy and the key material from our servers. Your device keeps everything.
  • To delete the account itself (the email, phone number or Google identifier you signed in with, and its Firebase user id), email appricity.support@gmail.com and we will remove it.
  • Bear in mind we cannot read the stored records, so we cannot export or correct them on your behalf — only delete them. Export lives in the app, where the data is readable.

9. Children

Spendly is a general-purpose finance tool and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect data from children. With sync off we collect nothing from anyone; with sync on, what we receive is listed in section 3.

10. Your rights

Privacy laws such as the GDPR (EEA/UK) and CCPA/CPRA (California) give you rights to access, correct, export and delete your personal data.

With sync off, you exercise all of these directly in the app: your data is on your device, you can export it at any time, and you can erase it completely. There is no request to submit because there is no server-side copy.

With sync on, the same is true of the readable data — it is on your device and under your control — and the additional server-side copy is covered in section 8: you can delete it from the app, and delete the account by email. We cannot provide access to or correction of the encrypted records, because we cannot read them; that is a consequence of the design rather than a refusal, and the readable original is always in your hands.

11. Changes to this policy

Optional encrypted sync arrived in version 1.1 and is documented above. If a future version introduces any further feature that transmits data — for example optional AI features that use a third-party model provider, analytics, or advertising — we will:

  1. Update this policy and its "Last updated" date before that version is released;
  2. Update the Google Play Data safety disclosure to match;
  3. Make any such feature opt-in, clearly labelled, and off by default;
  4. State plainly, in the app, exactly what data would leave your device;
  5. Where required (for example for advertising to users in the EEA, UK or Switzerland), obtain consent through a certified consent management platform and provide a way to withdraw that consent later.

Your existing financial records would remain on your device regardless.
