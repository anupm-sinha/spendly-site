Privacy Policy

Spendly for Android and iOS

Last updated: 16 August 2026

The short version

Spendly is an offline expense tracker. Your financial records — transactions, budgets, accounts, goals, notes, tags and receipt photos — are stored only on your device. We do not have a copy of them. There is no account to create and nothing to sign in to.

Spendly never sends your financial data anywhere. There is no sync, no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting.

The app makes exactly one kind of network request, and only if you switch it on: the optional Money reading section fetches public finance headlines from a few news publishers. It is off by default, it carries nothing about you, and your transactions are never involved. Everything else in Spendly — every calculation, every scan, every backup — works with no connection at all.

1. Who we are

Spendly is published by Appricity. For any privacy question or request, contact appricity.support@gmail.com.

2. What Spendly stores, and where

Everything you enter stays in a private database inside the app's own storage area on your device:

  • Transactions (expenses, income, transfers) including amounts, dates, notes, tags, merchant/payee names and split allocations
  • Categories, budgets, savings goals and accounts, including opening balances
  • Recurring rules
  • App preferences (theme, currency, haptics, App Lock, notification settings)
  • Receipt photos you choose to attach, stored as files in the app's own directory

We never receive any of this. It is not uploaded, backed up to our systems, or shared with anyone, because there is nowhere for it to go.

3. What we collect

Nothing. Spendly contains no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting, no telemetry and no tracking of any kind. We do not collect device identifiers, advertising IDs, IP addresses, usage statistics or diagnostics.

We therefore also do not sell or share personal information, and there is no profiling or automated decision-making.

(If you turn on the optional Money reading section, your device contacts news publishers directly to download their public headlines. Those publishers see the request the way any website does. Nothing about you is added to it, and nothing comes back to us — see section 6.)

5. Things you share into Spendly

You can hand Spendly a single item to save you typing:

  • Share a message to Spendly — from your messaging app, share a bank or payment alert. Spendly reads the amount, merchant, date and reference out of it on your device and shows you a draft to confirm.
  • Scan a receipt or screenshot — photograph a receipt, or pick a payment screenshot. The text is recognised on your device by a model built into the app, not by a service.
  • Paste text into Quick Add.

Three things are true of all of these:

  1. Spendly only ever sees the single item you hand it. It has no ability to read your inbox, your notifications or your photo library, and it does no background scanning. Nothing reaches Spendly unless you share it.
  2. Nothing is sent anywhere. Recognition and parsing happen entirely on your phone. Nothing you share or scan is ever transmitted, and the Money reading section (section 6) is completely separate from it — it only ever downloads public headlines and never uploads anything.
  3. Nothing is saved without you. Every capture is presented as a draft you confirm, edit or discard. Spendly never writes to your records on its own.

Images you scan are stored only if you keep them as a receipt attached to a transaction, in the app's own private folder, exactly like a receipt you attach by hand.

6. Money reading (optional, off by default)

Spendly can show a list of finance headlines from public news feeds. This is the only feature that uses the internet, and it is off until you turn it on in Settings → Money reading.

What is sent. A plain request for each publisher's public headline list — the same request your browser makes when it loads a page. It carries no account, no device identifier, no advertising ID, no cookies and none of your financial data. We do not add anything to it. As with any internet request, the publisher's server sees the IP address it came from; that is inherent to downloading anything and is not something Spendly collects, receives or stores.

What is stored. The headlines themselves are cached on your device so the section works offline. Turning the feature off deletes that cache.

Reading an article opens it in your own browser, not inside Spendly. From that point the publisher's own privacy policy applies — Spendly is not involved and cannot see what you read.

Current sources: Mint, Economic Times, Business Standard and MarketWatch.

We receive nothing from any of this. There is no server of ours in the path.

7. Files you create yourself

Spendly can export your data at your request: CSV export, JSON backup and PDF report are written to a temporary file and handed to your device's share sheet. CSV and backup import read from a file you pick.

Once you share a file, where it goes is determined by the app you send it to (email, cloud drive, messaging) and is governed by that app's privacy policy, not this one. We never see these files.

8. Data retention and deletion

Your data stays until you remove it. You are always in control:

  • Delete any individual record; it moves to Recently deleted and is purged automatically after 30 days, or immediately if you empty it
  • Settings → Erase all data permanently deletes everything
  • Uninstalling the app removes all of its local data

Because we hold no copy, there is nothing for you to request from us and nothing for us to delete on your behalf.

9. Children

Spendly is a general-purpose finance tool and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone — including children — because we do not collect data at all.

10. Your rights

Privacy laws such as the GDPR (EEA/UK) and CCPA/CPRA (California) give you rights to access, correct, export and delete your personal data. In Spendly you exercise all of these directly in the app: your data is on your device, you can export it at any time, and you can erase it completely. There is no request to submit because there is no server-side copy.

11. Changes to this policy

If a future version of Spendly introduces any feature that transmits data — for example optional cloud sync, optional AI features that use a third-party model provider, analytics, or advertising — we will:

  1. Update this policy and its "Last updated" date before that version is released;
  2. Update the Google Play Data safety disclosure to match;
  3. Make any such feature opt-in, clearly labelled, and off by default;
  4. State plainly, in the app, exactly what data would leave your device;
  5. Where required (for example for advertising to users in the EEA, UK or Switzerland), obtain consent through a certified consent management platform and provide a way to withdraw that consent later.

Your existing financial records would remain on your device regardless.
