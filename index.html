Spendly

Track every rupee, dollar and yen — privately, offline by default.

No sign-up required. No ads. Most expense apps want your bank login, your email address and a subscription. Spendly wants none of it — open the app and start tracking, no account needed. Everything stays on your device unless you turn on encrypted sync.

What it does

  • Track everything — expenses, income and transfers, with tags, merchants, receipt photos and split categories
  • Capture without typing — share a bank SMS to Spendly, or scan a paper receipt or payment screenshot. Read on your phone, never uploaded, and always shown to you as a draft to confirm
  • Quick add — type "coffee 4.50" and Spendly works out the rest
  • Budgets that help — monthly and per-category limits, a daily safe-to-spend allowance, rollover, and savings goals
  • Real insights — category breakdowns, six-month trends, top merchants, unusual-spending alerts and net worth over time
  • Accounts & automation — live balances, transfers between your own accounts, and recurring bills added when due
  • Optional finance headlines — Mint, Economic Times, Business Standard and MarketWatch, off by default
  • Yours to keep — CSV export, JSON backup, PDF reports, and import back any time

Privacy is the design, not a feature

Spendly has no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it works completely without an account. Your transactions, budgets and receipts stay on your phone unless you ask for otherwise.

Two features use the internet, and both are off until you switch them on:

Sync gives you a backup and a second device, and it is end-to-end encrypted. Every record is encrypted on your phone before it is uploaded, using a passphrase we never receive — so we cannot read your spending, and could not produce it if we were asked to. The flip side is worth saying out loud: forget that passphrase and the encrypted copy is gone for good. Local backups stay one tap away.

Money reading downloads public finance headlines. It sends nothing about you and never touches your financial data.

Spendly cannot read your messages, your notifications or your photo library, and asks for no permission to. When you share a bank alert or scan a receipt, Spendly sees only that one item, reads it on your phone, and shows you a draft to confirm.
